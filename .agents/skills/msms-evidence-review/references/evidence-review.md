# Evidence-review checklist

Apply the same checklist in direct and delegated reviews. Assess every result, including high-scoring candidates and no-candidate outcomes. Begin when a stable per-feature result is available; other features may still be running.

- Verify the feature, original experimental input, precursor/adduct, neutral formula hypotheses and their source, model/atlas/checkpoint provenance, and collision-energy values, units and acquisition mode. Keep inferred formulas distinct from supplied formulas. Record conflicts as unresolved input issues.
- Compare leading candidates against the full experimental spectra at every supplied collision energy. Check `energy_alignment` and the actual observed/predicted spectra, not only aggregate scores. For stepped acquisition, inspect the composite comparison and its component provenance; do not manufacture separate component observations.
- Build a candidate-specific table of peaks relevant to the decision: candidate ID/SMILES, experimental m/z, intensity, spectrum key and acquisition setting, matched/unmatched status, predicted assignment when available, and what the observation supports or contradicts. Include useful matches, strong unexplained peaks and peaks that distinguish competing candidates. A coincident m/z is not proof of a fragment structure or mechanism.
- Assess entropy similarity and explained intensity alongside unresolved peaks and candidate differences the data cannot distinguish. Repeated observations at different energies can support an interpretation, but they are not independent proofs. Avoid unsupported stereochemical or positional assignments. The 0.5 weak-match flag is a review heuristic, not calibrated confidence.
- Check candidate-search completeness and formula/adduct/energy consistency. Missing spectra or failed predictions require an operational fix and a provisional assessment, rather than a structural rejection or automatic de novo selection. Consider possible precursor leakage, isotopes, co-isolation or noise before treating unmatched peaks as structural requirements.
- Treat `review_evidence` as a starting summary. Verify its named `best_candidate` against the current ranking; its unexplained list includes only 20 peaks and may describe an earlier ranking. Reconstruct candidate-specific unmatched peaks from the complete observed spectra and match records. Record how an assignment was obtained and distinguish it from measured evidence.
- Use mirror spectra and fragment assignments where available. With numeric data alone, identify that limitation explicitly; do not claim visual or atom-level inspection that was not performed. Missing fragment structures do not erase a measured m/z match, but they limit structural interpretation.

Record supporting and conflicting observations, candidate-specific ambiguities, coverage, and a recommended next action in `evidence_review.json` using the [review format](review-format.md). Ground recommendations in named peaks or missing evidence, rather than a preferred identity or score threshold alone. Refresh the assessment after candidate/energy coverage or rankings change.

The coordinator summarizes weakly supported entries and asks whether to use agent proposals with model scoring, FRIGID with model scoring, both or skip. Reuse an explicit saved choice only when it covers the same analysis, entries and model context. A reviewer recommendation is not user authorization. Report current hypotheses and remaining uncertainty when no further route is selected.
